## Context

Shepwashi is a software development and AI consulting firm operating across Latin America and the Caribbean. It opened a new line of business: distributing service robots from an international manufacturer.

To launch the offer it needed a site of its own. I designed Shepwashi Robotics, a tech sub-brand that inherits the parent brand's identity.

## Challenge

The catalogue brings together 20 models across five lines (reception, last-mile, security, humanoid and medical) for 10 industries, from hotels and hospitals to factories, parcel delivery and elderly care.

These are high-value machines bought with expert advice, so the site doesn't close sales online. Its job is to generate qualified leads and, at the same time, present a new line with credibility.

## Approach

A distributor with 20 models competes on how well it organises its offer. That's why the catalogue lets visitors explore by line and compare, and every product page and every industry ends in a quote, an assessment or a conversation with an advisor.

For each robot I defined a fixed structure, running from what buyers ask first to what they ask later:

1. Four key figures that can be read in seconds.
2. Main functions.
3. Use scenarios.
4. Full technical sheet.
5. Related models.

The copy sticks to what the manufacturer backs. Where measurements were missing, I described capabilities instead of inventing figures, and track-record figures are attributed to the manufacturer.

## Solution

I led the project end to end and used AI as support for the wireframes.

- **Information architecture:** 38 screens on desktop and mobile.
- **Design system:** colour tokens with light and dark modes, the Geist typeface and reusable components.
- **Visual direction:** a cool grey base, Shepwashi's navy and a gold reserved for calls to action.
- **Interaction:** dropdown menus on desktop and mobile.
- **Imagery:** the manufacturer's product photos, cut out to float over a navy stage in the dark version.
- **Copy:** adapted to the region's Spanish.

In the catalogue, filters showing how many models each line has make the range visible at a glance, and a search box accepts either the model's name or the task that needs solving.

The result is a complete design system and 76 screens: 38 in light and 38 in dark. The site is in the design stage, with the prototype still to be wired up, so there are no usage metrics yet.

## Learnings

When a product is sold with expert advice, the site's goal isn't the cart but making every page lead to a good conversation.

A fixed structure per product page, ordered by the buyer's questions, makes 20 models easy to compare.

Being strict about what the manufacturer backs protects a new brand's credibility more than any eye-catching figure.
