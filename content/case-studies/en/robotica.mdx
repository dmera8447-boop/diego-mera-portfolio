## Context

Shepwashi opened a service robotics line: reception, last-mile, security, humanoid and medical robots, with implementation and support from Shepwashi. That line needed a site of its own, separate from the corporate one.

## Challenge

Nobody buys a service robot on impulse.

The typical visitor is a business with a concrete problem, such as welcoming visitors, moving orders or watching over a space, that doesn't know which model solves it. The site had to help them find it and request a demo.

## Approach

The hero talks about the outcome, not the technology: "Service robotics, ready to operate". The image shows a robot working in a real setting.

I organised the catalogue by task rather than by technical spec, because that's how buyers think.

## Solution

The catalogue presents 20 models across five lines. At the top, one sentence sums up the offer, and filters showing how many models each line has make the range visible at a glance.

A search box accepts either the model's name or the task that needs solving.

Each robot has a card with its photo, line, name and what it's for in one sentence. Contact stays visible in the navigation bar.

## Learnings

In a technical catalogue, naming each product by the job it does is what brings the sale closer.

Filtering by task and searching in everyday language shortened the distance between "I have this problem" and "this is the robot".
