## Context

Luntriqa is Shepwashi's product for insurers and brokers across Latin America. It's where claims are reported, reviewed and settled, and where policies, issuances and commissions are managed.

The platform already existed. In 2026 I led the full redesign of its management modules, in three weeks, alongside the PM and the development team. The flows, the interface and the design system were mine.

## Challenge

The system worked, but it was hard to use. The flows were hard to follow, the information was poorly organised and the product had no clear design system.

On top of that, three roles share the same platform with different needs:

- **Insurer:** manages claim states, subcategories and trends.
- **Broker:** follows their client portfolio, issuances and commissions.
- **Client:** checks their policies and tracks their claims.

## Approach

Since it was an internal product, users' needs and pain points came to me straight from the PM and the developers, who know each role's day-to-day.

The decision that took the most defending was unifying the roles under one navigation structure and one design system, instead of redesigning each module separately. It took longer at first, but it made the product more consistent and easier for development to maintain.

- One navigation that adapts to the role, instead of a separate screen for each profile.
- Components shared across modules, so development builds once and reuses.
- The first row of every dashboard answers the everyday questions before going into detail.

The shared design system made it possible to design Mr. I later without starting from scratch.

## Mr. I, the intelligence layer

Mr. I is an AI assistant for insurers. It connects to the modules through their statistics and is built for management, who need answers, not screens.

### Chat first

The core design decision was to start from the chat. Every answer arrives with visual data (indicators, charts, findings and actions), not just text.

### What I designed

- **Chat with visual answers:** an executive summary of the four modules, with recommendations.
- **Full-screen management dashboard:** filters only for period and module, and the key data from Claims, In-branch sales, Web sales and Optimisation. For example, the average time to resolve a claim against a 7-day target, and the cases outside it.
- **"Thinking" state:** a checklist of steps that shows what the AI is doing before it answers.
- **Empty states and themes:** new chat, dashboard without data, and light and dark mode.
- **Responsive:** desktop at 1440 and mobile at 393.

### Refinement

I removed the Luntriqa logo to keep only the Mr. I brand, and cut repeated icons, pills and cards so the product would feel calmer and easier to read.

I also removed the suggested questions, to leave a clean chat.

## Solution

The redesign shipped as part of Luntriqa and is being rolled out gradually with the development team.

The first visible effect: claim creation flows take less time to complete.

## Learnings

In a multi-role product, redesigning module by module is faster at first, but it multiplies inconsistencies. Investing first in a shared structure and system is what made it possible to deliver, in three weeks, something development can maintain.

Writing the conclusion next to the chart turned out to matter as much as the chart itself, especially for the profiles who don't live inside the numbers.

An AI assistant is only useful if it rests on data from the real modules: designing it after the shared system made it consistent with the rest of the product.
